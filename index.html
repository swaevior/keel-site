The philosophy

We don't make the calls. We follow where the math leads.

Nobody here picks trades or guesses where prices are going. My job is to set up fair tests and take out the two things that ruin most trading: human error and computer error. What survives the tests decides; I just make sure it's done right.

αSeeking alpha is finance-speak for hunting returns that come from a real edge, not from just riding the market up. It's rare, which is why most of our ideas end up in the "failed" pile, and why we keep looking.

Human error, removed
  • No gut feelings. Rules decide every trade. I only approve the rules, in advance.
  • No panic, no greed. The system can't sell in a fright or double down to win it back.
  • No wishful thinking. Pass rules are written before a test runs, so a result can't be bent to fit a hope.
Computer error, removed
  • Checked twice. Two separate machines run each day and must reach the same answer. Today they match.
  • Built twice. Anything heading to real money gets a second, independent version that has to match the first.
  • A separate referee. Safety rules live apart from the trading logic and can veto any order, with 380 tests behind them.

What could be wrong

The fine print, in large print

Good results deserve suspicion, ours most of all. These are the reasons to stay humble, and we keep them in view.

  1. Luck looks like skill. Test 136 versions and a few will shine by chance. Once we adjust for how many we tried, even our best results are less certain than the headline numbers.
  2. New data is harder. The crypto system scored a Sharpe ratio of 1.55 on the years it was designed on, and 0.47 on the years held back. Still positive, but a third of what it looked like.
  3. Famous ideas fade. The stock-timing ideas that passed are well known. Others may already be trading them away; one is visibly weakening.
  4. Practice is two days old. Nothing here is proof yet. The practice books need months before they mean anything.
  5. Tests aren't markets. We charge every test double the expected costs, but real trading can still surprise us.
How we keep it safe

Rules first, money later

Pass rules are written first

Every test has its finish line set before it starts. No moving the goalposts.

Hidden data for the final exam

The most recent data stays locked away until the very end, like an exam the system never studied for.

Three months of practice money

A system trades pretend money for at least three months and has to match its test before real money is considered.

Automatic circuit breakers

A bad day (down 10%) or a deep slide stops all new bets until I look. A kill switch stops everything.

Smaller bets when markets get wild

Position sizes shrink automatically when prices swing harder. Never bigger bets to win back a loss.

Everything is written down

Every decision the system makes is logged with the numbers behind it, so any surprise can be traced.
